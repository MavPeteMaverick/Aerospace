## Conservation of Mass (Continuity Equation)

Mass can neither be created nor destroyed. For a steady, one-dimensional flow through a streamtube or pipe, the mass flow rate ($\dot{m}$) must be constant at every cross-section.

$$
\dot{m} = \rho_1 A_1 V_1 = \rho_2 A_2 V_2 = \text{constant}
$$

If the flow is **incompressible** (density $\rho$ is constant), this simplifies to the volumetric flow rate:

$$
A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2
$$

## Conservation of Momentum (Euler to Bernoulli)

Applying Newton's Second Law ($F = ma$) to a fluid particle moving along a streamline, and assuming the flow is **steady, inviscid (frictionless), and incompressible**, we can integrate Euler's equation to yield the famous **Bernoulli Equation**:

$$
p + \frac{1}{2}\rho V^2 + \rho g z = \text{constant along a streamline}
$$

- $p$: Static pressure
- $\frac{1}{2}\rho V^2$: Dynamic pressure
- $\rho g z$: Hydrostatic pressure

<WatchOut title="Bernoulli Restrictions">
Never apply Bernoulli's equation blindly! It is ONLY valid for:
1. Steady flow
2. Incompressible flow ($Ma < 0.3$)
3. Inviscid flow (no friction/viscosity)
4. Along a single streamline (unless the flow is irrotational)
</WatchOut>

<WorkedExample title="Venturi Velocity">
Water ($\rho = 1000 \text{ kg/m}^3$) flows horizontally through a Venturi tube. At point 1, $A_1 = 0.1 \text{ m}^2$ and $V_1 = 2 \text{ m/s}$. At point 2, $A_2 = 0.05 \text{ m}^2$. What is the pressure difference $p_1 - p_2$?

**Step 1: Find $V_2$ using Continuity**
$$
V_2 = V_1 \frac{A_1}{A_2} = 2 \left( \frac{0.1}{0.05} \right) = 4 \text{ m/s}
$$

**Step 2: Find pressure diff using Bernoulli (horizontal, so $z_1 = z_2$)**
$$
p_1 + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_1^2 = p_2 + \frac{1}{2}\rho V_2^2
$$
$$
p_1 - p_2 = \frac{1}{2}\rho (V_2^2 - V_1^2) = \frac{1}{2}(1000)(4^2 - 2^2) = 500(16 - 4) = 6000 \text{ Pa}
$$
</WorkedExample>

<Recap>
- Mass flow rate $\dot{m} = \rho A V$ is constant for 1D steady flow.
- For incompressible flow, velocity increases as area decreases.
- The Bernoulli equation relates pressure, velocity, and elevation.
- Bernoulli is restricted to steady, incompressible, inviscid flow along a streamline.
</Recap>

<CheckUnderstanding>
  <Question prompt="If water flows into a nozzle that halves in area, what happens to the velocity?">
    Because water is essentially incompressible, the velocity must double to conserve mass flow rate ($A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2$).
  </Question>
  <Question prompt="Can you use Bernoulli's equation to find the pressure across a shockwave at Mach 2?">
    No. A shockwave involves highly compressible, viscous, and non-isentropic effects. Bernoulli's equation assumes incompressible, inviscid flow.
  </Question>
</CheckUnderstanding>

## Density and Pressure

At every point in a continuous fluid field, we can define properties.

**Density ($\rho$)** is mass per unit volume:
$$
\rho = \lim_{\delta \mathcal{V} \to \delta \mathcal{V}^*} \frac{\delta m}{\delta \mathcal{V}}
$$
where $\delta \mathcal{V}^*$ is the smallest volume where the continuum assumption still holds.

**Pressure ($p$)** is the normal force per unit area exerted by the fluid:
$$
p = \lim_{\delta A \to 0} \frac{\delta F_n}{\delta A}
$$

## Viscosity

Viscosity is a measure of a fluid's resistance to deformation (its "thickness" or internal friction).

- **Dynamic Viscosity ($\mu$)**: The proportionality constant relating shear stress ($\tau$) to the velocity gradient ($du/dy$) in a Newtonian fluid: $\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$.
- **Kinematic Viscosity ($\nu$)**: The ratio of dynamic viscosity to density:
$$
\nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho}
$$
Kinematic viscosity is often interpreted as the diffusivity of momentum.

### Temperature Dependence (Sutherland's Law)

<WatchOut title="Liquids vs. Gases">
In **liquids**, viscosity *decreases* as temperature increases (think of heating up syrup).
In **gases** (like air), viscosity *increases* as temperature increases because higher thermal energy means more molecular collisions transferring momentum between layers.
</WatchOut>

For air, we calculate dynamic viscosity at a given temperature $T$ using **Sutherland's Law**:

$$
\mu = \mu_{ref} \left( \frac{T}{T_{ref}} \right)^{3/2} \frac{T_{ref} + S}{T + S}
$$

Where for air:
- $\mu_{ref} = 1.716 \times 10^{-5} \text{ kg/(m}\cdot\text{s)}$
- $T_{ref} = 273.15 \text{ K}$
- $S = 110.4 \text{ K}$

<WorkedExample title="Viscosity at High Temp">
Calculate the dynamic viscosity of air at a stagnation temperature of $400 \text{ K}$.

Using Sutherland's law:
$$
\mu = 1.716 \times 10^{-5} \left( \frac{400}{273.15} \right)^{3/2} \frac{273.15 + 110.4}{400 + 110.4}
$$
$$
\mu \approx 2.3 \times 10^{-5} \text{ kg/(m}\cdot\text{s)}
$$
</WorkedExample>

<Recap>
- Density ($\rho$) is mass per volume.
- Pressure ($p$) is normal force per area.
- Dynamic viscosity ($\mu$) relates shear stress to velocity gradients.
- Kinematic viscosity ($\nu = \mu/\rho$) relates to momentum diffusion.
- Gas viscosity increases with temperature (Sutherland's Law), while liquid viscosity decreases.
</Recap>

  <Question prompt="What is the difference between dynamic and kinematic viscosity?">

  </Question>
  <Question prompt="If you heat up a tank of water and a tank of air, what happens to their respective viscosities?">
